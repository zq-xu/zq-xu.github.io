# Personal Pronouns and Verb Conjugation

## 1. Person Markers and Conjugation Patterns

Maltese verbs change according to the person and number of the subject. Pronouns can usually be omitted because the verb form often identifies the person; use them for emphasis or contrast. `hu` (he) and `hi` (she) distinguish masculine and feminine third-person singular. `inti` (you, singular) is used for any gender.

A common pattern is “person prefix + verb stem”: first person often uses `n-`, second person `t-`, and third-person masculine `j-`. Third-person feminine often has the same form as second-person singular. Plural forms commonly use a plural stem or the ending `-u`. For example, `kiteb` (write):

| Person | Pronoun | Common marker | Form of `kiteb` |
|---|---|---|---|
| First-person singular | jien | n- | nikteb |
| Second-person singular | inti | t- | tikteb |
| Third-person singular masculine | hu | j- | jikteb |
| Third-person singular feminine | hi | t- | tikteb |
| First-person plural | aħna | n- + plural stem | niktbu |
| Second-person plural | intom | t- + plural stem | tiktbu |
| Third-person plural | huma | j- + plural stem | jiktbu |

This is a common template, not a mechanical rule for every verb. Stem type and stem changes must be learned for each verb.

### Initial and Medial `għ`

- When a root begins with `għ`, the letters usually remain in the spelling after the person prefix, while the stem vowel may change: `għen` → `ngħin`, `għex` → `ngħix`.
- When `għ` occurs inside a root, it usually remains in the spelling; vowels or other parts of the stem may change: `bagħat` → `nibgħat`, `qagħad` → `noqgħod`, `biegħ` → `nbigħ`.
- Use “person prefix + the relevant stem pattern” as a guide. There is no single character-substitution formula for every `għ` verb; learn each verb's forms.

Maltese commonly omits a separate verb “to be” when stating identity, for example `Jien student.` (I am a student.)

## 3. Gender and Agreement

- `hu` and `hi` distinguish masculine and feminine; third-person verb forms may also differ, for example `jikteb` (he writes) and `tikteb` (she writes).
- Second-person singular `inti` is used for any gender; the verb forms in the examples above are the same.
- Nouns and adjectives may agree in gender and number: `tifel tajjeb` (a good boy), `tifla tajba` (a good girl).
- Plural verb forms often use their own endings. Do not generalize one verb's conjugation to every root.

## 4. Practice Patterns

Substitute a verb into the patterns below, then change the subject:

- `Jien ___ il-ħobż.`: I ___ bread.
- `Hi ___ l-ilma.`: She ___ water.
- `Aħna ___ id-dar.`: We ___ home / at home.

Learn a verb form together with an example. For irregular verbs and loan verbs, consult a full conjugation table.
