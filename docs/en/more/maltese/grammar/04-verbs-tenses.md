# Verbs and Basic Tenses

This chapter is organized as “rules → tense-specific conjugation → comparison.” Maltese commonly uses the imperfect to express present, habitual, or ongoing actions; the perfect expresses completed actions; and the future is often formed with `se + imperfect`. These forms do not map one-to-one onto English tenses; context and time expressions also matter.

## 1. Three Common Constructions

| Learning label | Maltese form | Common use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present/habitual: imperfect | person form + verb stem | Current state, habitual action, or an action not yet complete | `Jien nikteb.`: I write / I am writing. |
| Past: perfect | person form of the perfect | Completed action or event | `Jien ktibt.`: I wrote. |
| Future | `se + imperfect` | Planned, predicted, or future action | `Jien se nikteb.`: I will write. |

The imperfect and perfect are form/aspect categories, not exact equivalents of English present and past. Context and time expressions help establish the time. Person forms are explained in [Personal Pronouns and Verb Conjugation](03-person-conjugation.md).

## 2. Imperfect and Perfect

The Maltese verb system commonly uses the terms “imperfect” and “perfect” for forms/aspects. As a beginner approximation, the imperfect can express present, habitual, or unfinished actions, while the perfect can express completed actions (often corresponding to the past). This is not a one-to-one mapping with English tenses. Maltese has no infinitive that corresponds exactly to English `to eat`.

## 3. Present or Habitual Actions: Imperfect

Verb forms change by person and may also change their endings. Using `kiteb` (write) as an example:

| Person | Malti | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| I | nikteb | I write / I am writing |
| you (singular) | tikteb | you write / you are writing |
| he | jikteb | he writes / he is writing |
| she | tikteb | she writes / she is writing |
| we | niktbu | we write / we are writing |
| you (plural) | tiktbu | you write / you are writing |
| they | jiktbu | they write / they are writing |

Examples:

- `Jien nikteb ittra.`: I write a letter.
- `Hi taqra ktieb.`: She reads a book.
- `Aħna naħdmu hawn.`: We work here.

Context or a time expression usually indicates whether an action is happening right now; the imperfect does not only mean “currently in progress.”

## 4. Completed Actions: Perfect

Using `kiteb` (write), the perfect commonly describes a completed past action:

| Person | Malti | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| I | ktibt | I wrote |
| you (singular) | ktibt | you wrote |
| he | kiteb | he wrote |
| she | kitbet | she wrote |
| we | ktibna | we wrote |
| you (plural) | ktibtu | you wrote |
| they | kitbu | they wrote |

Examples:

- `Ilbieraħ ktibt ittra.`: Yesterday I wrote a letter.
- `Hu kiel il-ħobż.`: He ate the bread.
- `Morna r-ristorant.`: We went to the restaurant.

Perfect forms involve stem changes; do not mechanically apply one verb's forms to all verbs. See [Personal Pronouns and Verb Conjugation](03-person-conjugation.md) for more person forms.

## 5. Future

A common construction places the future marker `se` (also `ser`) or `ħa` before the imperfect. Time words such as `għada` (tomorrow) can also indicate the future.

- `Għada se mmur Malta.`: Tomorrow I will go to Malta.
- `Se nikteb l-ittra.`: I will write the letter.
- `Ħa nieklu issa.`: We are going to eat now.

Usage of `se`, `ser`, and `ħa` varies with colloquial habit and context. Beginners can start with `se + imperfect`.

## 6. Introductory Negation and Questions

- A common negative frame is `ma ... x`: `Ma nafx.` (I don't know); `Ma nifhimx.` (I don't understand). Individual verbs may show form changes.
- Yes/no questions can be formed through intonation or context: `Titkellem bl-Ingliż?` (Do you speak English?)
- Question words often come first: `Fejn tgħix?` (Where do you live?)

## 7. Study Tips

First learn the common imperfect forms of “write,” then practise `jiekol` (eat), `jaqra` (read), and `jaħdem` (work) in simple sentences. For each new verb, learn one first-person form and one he/she form.
