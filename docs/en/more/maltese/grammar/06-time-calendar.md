# Telling the Time

Maltese uses both traditional spoken clock expressions and numeric 24-hour time. Numeric notation is clearest when arranging a meeting. In spoken expressions, note the forms of `siegħa` (hour) and the clock numerals.

## 1. Asking the Time and Saying O'Clock

- `X'ħin hu?`: What time is it?
- `Huwa s-sagħtejn.`: It is two o'clock.
- `Hija s-siegħa.`: It is one o'clock.
- `Huma t-tlieta.`: It is three o'clock.

Common o'clock forms are below. Noon and midnight are also commonly expressed as `nofsinhar` and `nofsillejl`; articles and colloquial forms may vary slightly by context.

| Time | English | Common Malti |
|---|---|---|
| 01:00 | one o'clock | is-siegħa |
| 02:00 | two o'clock | is-sagħtejn |
| 03:00 | three o'clock | it-tlieta |
| 04:00 | four o'clock | l-erbgħa |
| 05:00 | five o'clock | il-ħamsa |
| 06:00 | six o'clock | is-sitta |
| 07:00 | seven o'clock | is-sebgħa |
| 08:00 | eight o'clock | it-tmienja |
| 09:00 | nine o'clock | id-disgħa |
| 10:00 | ten o'clock | l-għaxra |
| 11:00 | eleven o'clock | il-ħdax |
| 12:00 | noon / midnight | nofsinhar / nofsillejl |

Ask `X'ħin hu?`. In response, give the clock expression or numeric time, for example `Huwa s-sagħtejn.` (It is two o'clock) or `Huwa 14:00.` (It is 14:00).

## 3. Hours and Minutes

`u` means “and/past” and is used for minutes after the hour: `it-tlieta u għaxar minuti` (ten past three). `nofs` means “half”: `it-tlieta u nofs` (half past three). `neqsin` is the plural of `nieqes` (missing/short) and expresses minutes to the next hour: `it-tlieta neqsin kwart` (a quarter to three, 2:45). Numeric time is clearest for minutes that are not a quarter-hour. Before a time, `f'` is commonly used and contracts with the article according to spelling conventions.

- `it-tlieta u kwart`: quarter past three (3:15)
- `it-tlieta u nofs`: half past three (3:30)
- `it-tlieta neqsin kwart`: quarter to three (2:45)
- `l-erbgħa neqsin għaxar minuti`: ten to four (3:50)
- `fit-08:15`: at 08:15
- `fit-14:30`: at 14:30
- `fit-18:45`: at 18:45

For hours and minutes, say the hour first and then the minutes. If unsure about the traditional spoken form, use 24-hour numeric notation. Numeric notation is also recommended for formal appointments, tickets, and itineraries.

## 5. Durations

Use `għal + duration` to express “for a duration”; `idum` (to last/take) can also state how long something takes. Minutes are `minuta/minuti`; `kwarta` means a quarter of an hour, and `nofs siegħa` means half an hour. `sagħtejn` is a common dual form for two hours; `sigħat` is used for multiple hours. Two days can be expressed as `jumejn`.
