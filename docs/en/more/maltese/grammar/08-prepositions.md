# Common Prepositions and Their Uses

Prepositions are used with nouns and definite articles; some contract with the following article. Prepositions can also take person suffixes, creating forms such as “to me,” “with you,” or “at his place.” The list is ordered approximately by everyday frequency. Dictionaries may also list rare dialectal, archaic, conjunction-like, and inflected forms; this chapter focuses on useful prepositions and phrases in modern standard Maltese.

Common contractions include `f' / fi`, `b' / bi`, `ma'`, `għal`, `minn`, `ta'`, `sa`, `lil`, `bħal`, and `ġo`. In phrases such as `faċċata ta'` and `barra minn`, the `ta'` or `minn` element contracts. Most other prepositions remain separate from the definite article. The `il-` and `l-` columns sometimes produce the same result in contracted forms; both are shown to compare the requested article spellings, not to imply separate rules.

## 3. Direction, Origin, and Location

- `immur Malta`: I am going to Malta. (Place names do not always require an article.)
- `niġi minn Malta`: I come from Malta.
- `Il-ktieb qiegħed fuq il-mejda.`: The book is on the table.
- `Il-qattus qiegħed taħt is-sodda.`: The cat is under the bed.
- `L-iskola tinsab ħdejn id-dar.`: The school is next to the house.

## 4. Prepositions of Time

- `filgħodu`: in the morning; `wara nofsinhar`: in the afternoon; `filgħaxija`: in the evening.
- `f'Ġunju`: in June.
- `nhar it-Tnejn`: on Monday.
- `qabel l-ikel`: before the meal; `wara l-ikel`: after the meal.

## 5. `għand` for “Have”

Maltese commonly uses a person form of `għand` to express possession rather than an English-style “have” verb:

| English | Malti |
|---|---|
| I have | għandi |
| you have (singular) | għandek |
| he has | għandu |
| she has | għandha |
| we have | għandna |
| you have (plural) | għandkom |
| they have | għandhom |

Examples: `Għandi karozza.` (I have a car.) `Għandek kelb?` (Do you have a dog?)

## 6. Study Tips

Learn each preposition together with a noun rather than memorizing only one English gloss; `f'`, `għal`, and `minn` can express different relationships depending on context. Notice contractions with articles, such as `fil-`, `bil-`, `mal-`, `għall-`, `mill-`, and `tal-`.
