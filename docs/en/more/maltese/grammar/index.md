# Maltese for Beginners

These notes are for beginner learners. Maltese is written with the standard Latin alphabet. English glosses are intended as quick references; meanings may vary with context and collocation.

## Learning Materials

1. [Editorial requirements](00-requirements.md): the scope and original requirements for this learning resource.
2. [Categorized basic vocabulary](01-vocabulary.md): ten core categories, with at least 20 entries each. Family, home, animals, plants, transport, and time include relevant singular/plural forms; the animal table also notes male and female terms. Time is ordered from hours to days, weeks, months, and years. Question words and prepositions are ordered approximately by frequency.
3. [200 useful phrases](02-phrases.md): ten situations with 20 phrases each, covering greetings, politeness, conversation, food, shopping, travel, time, everyday needs, and emergencies.
4. [Personal pronouns and verb conjugation](03-person-conjugation.md): rules first, followed by five common verbs and five verbs each with initial and medial `għ`, fully conjugated for seven persons. Includes example sentences; tense is not covered.
5. [Verbs and basic tenses](04-verbs-tenses.md): imperfect, perfect, and future constructions, with verb tables for all seven persons and trilingual examples.
6. [Numbers and numeral grammar](05-numbers.md): cardinal numbers, compound numbers, 20 ordinal numbers, and ten large-number examples.
7. [Telling the time](06-time-calendar.md): o'clock, parts of the day and relative dates, clock times, and durations. Includes the uses of `u`, `nofs`, and `neqsin`, plus 20 time-of-day and 20 duration examples.
8. [Dates, weekdays, months, and seasons](07-dates.md): date expressions, calendar vocabulary, relative week/month/year expressions, and 20 examples.
9. [Common prepositions](08-prepositions.md): 44 useful prepositions and collocations, comparison with five article forms, notes on contraction, and example sentences.

## Suggested Study Order

Start with the alphabet and categorized vocabulary, then practise the 200 phrases by situation. Continue with pronouns and conjugation, tenses, numbers, time, dates, and prepositions. Choose five to ten words a day, substitute them into the example sentences, and read them aloud.

## Notes

- `ċ`, `ġ`, `ħ`, and `ż` are distinct Maltese letters. Preserve the spellings of `għ` and `ie` as well.
- Regional and colloquial variation exists. These notes prioritize common standard written forms; some expressions have other natural alternatives.
- Maltese verbs do not have an infinitive that corresponds exactly to English `to + verb`. The vocabulary list uses common citation forms; see the conjugation tables for person-specific forms.
