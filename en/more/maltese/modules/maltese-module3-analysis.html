Module 3: When Things Go Wrong ​

Module title: Meta l-affarijiet ma jmorrux kif nixtiequ
English title: When things go wrong

Module Title Breakdown ​

MalteseEnglishWord-by-word breakdown
Meta l-affarijiet ma jmorrux kif nixtiequWhen things don't go as we wishMeta when
l-affarijiet things (article l- + affarijiet)
ma jmorrux they don't go well (ma … -x forms the negative; positive form jmorru, literally "they go")
kif as, the way
nixtiequ we wish, we want (first person plural).

The English title When things go wrong is close to "when things go wrong"; the Maltese title reads more literally as "when things don't go as we wish". This module practises expressing yourself when things go wrong, through situations such as losing things, being late, accidents, and service complaints.

Reading Notes ​

  • il-/l- definite article, similar to English the; in context it can be translated "this/that" or left untranslated.
  • fil- in the (preposition + article, written together).
  • tal- of the (preposition + article, written together).
  • bil- with/by the (preposition + article, written together).
  • plural sħiħ regular/full plural, usually formed by adding an ending.
  • plural miksur broken plural; the stem changes internally and cannot be derived by a single uniform rule.

Correspondence with the Page 12 Vocabulary ​

  • fuq on: picture 1.
  • taħt under: picture 7.
  • quddiem in front of: picture 4.
  • wara behind: picture 5; the difference from picture 3 is that the apple is partly hidden by the box.
  • bejn between: picture 6; the plural il-kaxxi is used because the reference objects are two boxes.
  • ħdejn / maġenb next to: picture 3 could also be It-tuffieħa qiegħda ħdejn il-kaxxa., but that doesn't say left or right.
  • fuq in-naħa tal-lemin on the right side: the more specific expression for picture 3. The whole phrase means "on the right side"; don't translate only the fuq in it as "above".
  • fuq in-naħa tax-xellug on the left side: picture 4 is also somewhat left in the frame, so one could add It-tuffieħa qiegħda fuq in-naħa tax-xellug tal-kaxxa.; however, since it covers the front of the box, quddiem is the more accurate main relation.
  • fil-kaxxa in the box: picture 2, and provisionally picture 8; not "on top of the box".
  • madwar around: picture 9; distinct from bejn (between two or more reference objects).

Still to check: the box boundaries and the apple overlap in picture 8 are unclear; "inside" is not treated as the only certain answer. Picture 9 uses the collective noun tuffieħ rather than the single apple tuffieħa, so qiegħed is used accordingly.
